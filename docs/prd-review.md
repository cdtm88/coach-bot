# PRD v2 Review

Review of `docs/prd.md` (PRD v2) read against `docs/memory-design.md` and `docs/setup.md`.

Scope of this review: internal consistency, cross-document conflicts, phase coverage, testability of acceptance criteria, and gaps that would block implementation.

> **Status: resolved in PRD v2.1.** Every blocking, significant, minor and editorial finding below has been applied across `docs/prd.md`, `docs/memory-design.md` and `docs/setup.md`. Three findings resolved into the open items register rather than into requirements, because they need information the documents do not yet hold: S9 (wellness payload completeness, open item 3), S12 (persona seed artefact, open item 9) and S15 (HealthBridge ownership, open item 2). S8 is closed: the caps are USD 3.00 daily as a hard stop and USD 60 monthly as an alert, deliberately not proportional because the daily figure is a runaway backstop rather than a budget slice. This document stays as the record of what was wrong and why it was changed; the requirements themselves live in the PRD.
>
> Decisions taken where the review offered options: gym load converts onto the cycling scale via RPE × duration × a configured coefficient (GYM-08); `decay_days` is a half life with a 0.20 confidence floor; design section 4 is scoped to the memory subsystem rather than extended to the ingest tables.

Every finding below is stated as it was found, in the present tense of the pre-v2.1 documents.

**Headline.** The PRD is unusually strong on the things that normally go wrong: the governing asymmetry is stated once and inherited everywhere, acceptance criteria are attached to every requirement, and the absence-trap reasoning (FIT-12, RECOV-06, ADJ-08, CHAT-09) is consistent across four domains. The problems are concentrated in three places: the design document is stale relative to PRD v2 in ways that break P00, a small number of requirements contradict each other outright, and several load-bearing numbers are never defined.

Counts check out: 176 requirements, and the per-domain totals sum correctly.

## 1. Blocking — resolve before P00

### B1. No legal write path exists for safety facts

SAFE-02 says consolidation cannot create, change or supersede a safety key, and that "only an explicit athlete statement can". CONS-06 says in-turn writes land in `pending_writes` and are "only ratified by consolidation", with acceptance "No path exists from a chat turn to a direct facts insert."

Taken together, no path exists to ever write a safety fact after the initial seed. The athlete's statement can only reach `pending_writes`; consolidation is the only ratifier; consolidation is forbidden from touching safety keys. Design §6 step 3 confirms the block ("any write to a `safety` key [is] rejected and logged").

The design anticipates the missing path — `fact_events.actor` includes `athlete` — but no requirement specifies it. This is the most serious defect in the spec: SAFE-01 constraints are the highest-stakes facts in the system, and an athlete reporting a new injury mid-block has no way to get it recorded.

Proposed: add SAFE-06 defining an explicit athlete-confirmation path (actor `athlete`) that bypasses consolidation, including how the system distinguishes an explicit constraint statement from ordinary conversation, what confirmation the athlete sees, and the fact that this is the one exception to CONS-06. Then narrow CONS-06's acceptance to "no path exists from an ordinary chat turn to a direct facts insert."

### B2. Nightly wipe of working memory destroys the continuity note

MEM-09: working memory is "rewritten per turn and cleared nightly". Design §2 tier 1 is blunter: the conversation state row is "Wiped by the nightly job." But that row holds `open_threads`, `rolling_summary` and `last_topic`, and CHAT-05 requires the conversation to open from the continuity note referencing "the last open thread when one exists". Design §3 loads a 200-token continuity note on every turn.

If the row is wiped, CHAT-05 fails every morning. MEM-09's acceptance only checks `today_uncommitted` is empty, which suggests the intent is a partial clear — but the design says wipe, and the design is binding on memory semantics.

Proposed: MEM-09 states explicitly which columns clear (`today_uncommitted`) and which are regenerated by consolidation (`rolling_summary`, `open_threads`, `last_topic`); design §2 updated to match in the same change per SPEC-02.

### B3. Design §4 is not the full DDL the PRD says it is

The PRD's own governance table asserts design §4 carries "Full DDL for every table" and governs MEM, CONS, PLAN, ADJ and OBS. SPEC-01's acceptance is that "a reviewer can map every table and column in the implementation to the design document."

§4 defines seven tables: `fact_keys`, `facts`, `fact_events`, `notes`, `prescriptions`, `adjustment_events`, `conversation_state`, `pending_writes`, `feeds`, `recall_tests`. It does not define `sessions`, `blocks` (or block versions), the derived rollups table, messages, macros, wellness or calendar events — all of which are required by MEM-08, FIT-*, HLTH-02, RECOV-02, CALR-01, BLOCK-01 and OBS-01.

Concretely, this breaks the P00 migration: `prescriptions.session_id bigint references sessions(id)` and `prescriptions.block_id` reference tables that do not exist in the document. SPEC-01's acceptance is unmeetable as written.

Proposed: either extend §4 to cover the remaining tables, or amend the governance table and SPEC-01 to say §4 is authoritative for the memory subsystem tables only, with the ingest tables defined at their own phase. The second is cheaper and honest; the first is better if SPEC-01's reviewability promise matters.

### B4. Design §13 contradicts the PRD phase plan

Design §13 "Phase 00 acceptance criteria" bundles work the PRD assigns to four different phases into P00: item 1 is CHAT-01 (P01), item 3 is CONS-01 (P02), item 7 is OBS-02 (P12), and item 9 is the full mid-week adjustment loop — ADJ-01, ADJ-02, ADJ-06, ADJ-07 — which the PRD places in P09, four milestones later.

Precedence says the PRD wins on phase completion, so §13 loses. But it is left stale, which SPEC-02 explicitly forbids, and it is the section an implementer working from the design would read first.

Proposed: replace design §13 with a pointer to the PRD phase plan, or restate it as P00-only (schema, invariants, audit trail).

### B5. Whoop OAuth is forbidden by the PRD and still live in both companion docs

RECOV-03 requires that no direct Whoop integration exists and that the codebase contain no Whoop API client. SEC-04 requires that no OAuth flow exists anywhere.

Design §14 records as *resolved*: "Whoop developer platform access is free... v2 API, OAuth 2.0, webhooks available. App approval is only needed for public launch, not for single user personal use." Setup step 6 goes further and makes it a contingency: "If a metric the coach relies on is missing, that is the trigger for adding the direct Whoop integration."

Both are v1-era positions that PRD v2 has overruled. Leaving them in means the documented remedy for a real, likely failure (see S9) is a route the PRD bans.

Proposed: strike the direct-Whoop contingency from setup step 6 and mark design §14's Whoop bullet as superseded by RECOV-03/SEC-04, and replace the contingency with a permitted one — degrade gracefully on the missing metric, or accept the gap.

### B6. The governance section contradicts itself

§1 states: "this PRD wins on scope and acceptance." The table immediately below it in §1 states that design §10 *governs* FIT, HLTH, RECOV, CALR, PLAN, ADJ and LOG.

Design §10 is not confined to schema or memory semantics — it contains the trigger rule table, the bounds on automatic changes, the planning write rules and the feed staleness thresholds. That is scope, and the PRD restates much of it as ADJ-01 to ADJ-08 and PLAN-01 to PLAN-12. If the two ever diverge, §1's precedence rule and §1's mapping table give opposite answers.

This matters because SPEC-04's acceptance is "a seeded conflict is resolved by the stated rule rather than by judgement" — and the stated rule is ambiguous exactly where a conflict is most likely.

Proposed: change the mapping table's verb from "Governs" to "Informs" for design §10, or carve §10 explicitly out of the design's precedence and mark it as rationale rather than binding.

## 2. Significant — resolve before the affected phase

### S1. Four requirements are in no phase

| Requirement | Where it should live | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| FIT-17 (coach-authored activity marker) | P03 | P03 lists "FIT-01 to FIT-16"; FIT-17 was appended out of numeric order between FIT-12 and FIT-13 and the range was never updated. |
| PLAN-12 (athlete edit updates local prescription) | P08 | P08 lists "PLAN-01 to PLAN-11". |
| SEC-02 (webhook/ingest secret verification) | P03 and P04 | Overlaps FIT-02 and HLTH-01 but is never phase-checked. |
| SEC-03 (Telegram allowlist) | P01 | Overlaps CHAT-01 but is never phase-checked. |

Because "done when" is defined per phase, an unassigned requirement is never verified by anything.

### S2. Nothing arbitrates the total interruption budget

Six separate rules each grant one interruption, and none of them knows about the others:

- CONS-08: at most one verification candidate per conversation
- Design §8 mention-once: at most one pending mention per conversation
- HLTH-11: one light question to confirm an outlier reading
- HLTH-15: one mention when body mass is >12 days stale
- CHAT-09: stale feed surfaced so the agent asks
- REV-03: one question in the Sunday review

A single conversation could legitimately carry a verification candidate, a mention, an outlier question and a staleness nudge — four interruptions — while each individual rule reports compliance. That is precisely the interrogation the design's principle 7 and CHAT-04 exist to prevent, and it will read worse than any single violation.

Proposed: add a CHAT requirement defining one interruption budget per conversation with a priority order (safety > outlier confirmation > pending mention > verification candidate > staleness), and make the acceptance count total interruptions rather than per-category ones.

### S3. The combined load ceiling has no unit

GYM-05 requires gym sessions to count toward the weekly load ceiling alongside cycling, with acceptance "a week at the cycling ceiling cannot add a gym session without reducing elsewhere." GYM-04 requires gym load to be tracked as session count and mean RPE, explicitly *not* tonnage. BLOCK-07 caps the week-on-week planned load increase at a configured percentage, and ADJ-02 forbids any automatic change that increases load.

There is no defined unit in which a cycling session and a gym session are commensurable, so none of these three requirements is implementable or testable as written. BLOCK-07's acceptance ("generation is rejected if the limit is breached") cannot be evaluated at all for a mixed week.

Proposed: define the load unit explicitly — an RPE-and-duration to load-score mapping for gym, or a per-discipline ceiling with a stated exchange rate — as a requirement in the BLOCK or GYM domain.

### S4. Confidence floor and decay function are undefined

CONS-07 requires confidence to decay "by category half life", never reaching zero. Design §12's contradiction linter asserts "no active fact below the confidence floor being presented as certain."

Neither the floor nor the decay function is defined anywhere. Separately, the design is inconsistent about what `decay_days` means: the DDL column is `decay_days`, the §5 table header is "Decay", the §5 values read as lifetimes (365, 90, 30, 120, 180, 42), and §8 prose says "past its half life". A half-life of 30 days and a lifetime of 30 days produce very different confidence at day 90 — and CONS-07's acceptance turns on exactly that ("after simulated 90 days... present with reduced confidence").

Proposed: state the decay function and the floor as a requirement, and rename or annotate `decay_days` in the design so its semantics are unambiguous.

### S5. Four different bars for "the weight trend is trustworthy"

- HLTH-07: 3 readings before any directional claim
- HLTH-08: 6 readings spanning 3+ weeks before a rate of loss, always as a range
- HLTH-16: 4 weeks of readings before a programme change on weight evidence
- NUT-04: "at least 4 weekly readings" before the trend arbitrates against energy balance

NUT-04's phrasing is genuinely ambiguous — four readings, or four weeks of readings? Given HLTH-05 targets 2–3 readings per week, four readings is under two weeks, which would make NUT-04 a *weaker* bar than HLTH-08 for a stronger claim. Reading it as four weeks makes it consistent with HLTH-16.

Proposed: one table in the HLTH section giving claim type → minimum readings → minimum span, with NUT-04 referencing it rather than restating a threshold.

### S6. The ingest timing budget does not add up

PERF-03 caps file arrival → session review at 5 minutes. FIT-01's acceptance allows 5 minutes just for the session row to appear, and FIT-06 allows a further 5 minutes from ingest to the review. The stated sub-budgets sum to 10 minutes against a 5-minute total.

Proposed: pick one end-to-end figure and express FIT-01 and FIT-06 as sub-budgets within it.

### S7. NOTIF-02 does not inherit the absence-trap guard

FIT-12, RECOV-06, ADJ-08 and the design's "absence trap" all require that a missing activity be cross-checked against the recovery and load signal, and that nothing be concluded before the 18-hour grace window. NOTIF-02 fires a follow-up at 21:00 local with no such guard.

The failure is a follow-up asking about a session the athlete actually rode but has not synced — the exact false accusation the rest of the spec is built to avoid. It also sits oddly with HLTH-12's principle that a missed input is never characterised as a lapse.

Proposed: NOTIF-02 checks the load signal before firing, and its wording is specified as an offer rather than a chase.

### S8. The daily spend cap has no value, and three documents describe three different controls

OBS-07 requires "a daily spend cap [that] enforces a hard stop, independent of any monthly alert". PRD open item 5 asks for a "monthly cost ceiling to enforce". Setup step 5 says set a "monthly budget alert".

A hard stop with an undefined threshold is a self-inflicted outage waiting to happen — and nothing specifies what the athlete experiences when it trips, beyond OBS-07's "notifies". MODEL-03's fallback to the heavier model on router failure makes an unbounded-spend path more likely, not less.

Proposed: set the daily cap value, reconcile open item 5 with OBS-07, and add a requirement for degraded behaviour once the cap trips (the coach should say it is capped, not go silent).

### S9. RECOV-02 is unverifiable until setup step 6, and its failure has no permitted remedy

RECOV-02 requires six wellness fields per day. Setup step 6 correctly says to check what the wellness payload actually contains before assuming it covers everything. If a field is absent, the setup guide's remedy is the direct Whoop integration — which RECOV-03 and SEC-04 forbid (see B5).

This is a live risk, not a hypothetical: respiration and SpO2 are the least likely to survive the Whoop → intervals.icu hop, and RECOV-04's locally computed deviation is the thing that depends on them.

Proposed: promote this to a numbered open item with a resolution deadline before P05, and pre-agree the fallback (drop the missing field from RECOV-02 and from the deviation calculation).

### S10. The phase plan's critical path is mostly waiting

Several "done when" gates are real-elapsed-time gates rather than test gates:

- P01: "a week of real conversation"
- P06: "observed availability facts appear after two weeks of real calendar data" — and P06 gates P07 through P10
- P10: "four consecutive Sunday reviews"
- P12: "recall suite passes 30 of 30 for fourteen consecutive nights"

That is roughly two months of pure waiting on the critical path, with no way to proceed on seeded data. CONS-07 and SAFE-03 already show the pattern that works ("after simulated 90 days", "after simulated 365 days").

Proposed: split each phase's done-when into *implemented* (passes on seeded/simulated data, unblocks the next phase) and *validated* (confirmed over N weeks in production, tracked separately). Only P12's soak should genuinely gate anything.

### S11. MEM-11's measurement boundary is undefined and the headroom is thin

MEM-11 caps assembled context at 4,000 tokens p95 "excluding conversation history". Design §3 sums to 2,300 tokens preloaded and up to 3,800 with a 1,500-token episodic recall.

Two problems. First, it is unstated whether tool-call returns count toward the budget — episodic recall arrives as a tool result, so on the strict reading MEM-11 measures only the 2,300 and the cap is never at risk; on the loose reading the worst case is 3,800 against a 4,000 cap, about 5% headroom. Second, the design documents two expansion paths that blow through it anyway: "Full block on request" against a 700-token budget line, and episodic recall's 1,500-token ceiling being a soft one.

Proposed: state whether tool results count, and add a requirement for what gets dropped first when the budget is exceeded — with SAFE-01 constraints explicitly exempt, since they load verbatim and are never summarised.

### S12. The persona seed artefact is not in the repository

CHAT-02 and design §9 both require the persona to be seeded from "the July 2026 coaching conversation". Setup step 10 asks the athlete to review the extracted persona before loading, calling it the thing that "sets the tone for years". That conversation is not in the repo, and SPEC-03 requires the documents that matter to be readable during every phase.

P01 cannot start without it.

Proposed: commit the source conversation (or the extracted persona prompt) to the repo, and reference it by path from CHAT-02.

### S13. The two open-item registers have diverged

The PRD lists five open items. The design lists five bullets, two marked resolved, of which two — raw message retention/pruning, and the model split — appear nowhere in the PRD. Transcription appears in both. The design's "resolved" Whoop bullet contradicts the PRD outright (B5).

Raw message retention is the notable orphan: nothing in the PRD says how long messages are kept or whether consolidated days can be pruned, and OBS-06's backup requirement plus multi-year session streams make it a real decision.

Proposed: one open-items register in the PRD; the design links to it.

### S14. MEM-08 and CHAT-06 disagree on whether the agent sees session rows

MEM-08's acceptance: "the agent never receives raw session rows for arithmetic." CHAT-06 requires a `get_sessions` tool, and design §2 tier 3 says event tables are "reached through tools, never preloaded" — i.e. the agent does receive session rows.

The intent is clearly that aggregates come from SQL rollups and never from the model summing rows, but as written MEM-08 forbids a tool the PRD requires two sections later.

Proposed: reword MEM-08's acceptance to target the computation, not the data — no rollup value in any response originates from model arithmetic over session rows.

### S15. HealthBridge is load-bearing and unowned

Open item 1 turns on HealthBridge ("HealthBridge then proceeds as specified" / "can be dropped"), and design §10's body mass feed depends on it. But HealthBridge lives inside MacroLog, no requirement covers it, and setup §1's division-of-labour table has no row for MacroLog-side work.

If open item 1 resolves toward "build it", nothing in the PRD says who builds it, when, or what "as specified" means — it is specified nowhere.

Proposed: either state explicitly that HealthBridge is out of scope for this repo and a prerequisite of P04, or add the requirement.

## 3. Minor and editorial

**Weak or gameable acceptance criteria.** Several acceptances test a proxy rather than the requirement:

- CHAT-04 / LOG-04 ("no two question marks in separate sentences") — passes for two questions in one sentence, and for questions phrased as imperatives. The requirement is good; the test is not.
- CHAT-03 (no statement that something was "saved, noted or remembered") — keyword matching will miss most real violations. Worth specifying the regression prompt set, as SAFE-05 does.
- MEM-06 ("count of fact_events ≥ count of facts") — satisfied by one fact with ten events and nine facts with none. Should assert per-row: every fact has a created event, every superseded fact has a superseded event.
- MEM-10 ("no vector search occurs") — proving the absence of a technology rather than the presence of the behaviour. The first clause ("context contains all active facts") is the real test.

**Other items:**

- FIT-17 breaks numeric ordering (sits between FIT-12 and FIT-13). Cosmetic, but it is the visible trace of the S1 gap.
- OBS-05 says "all five feeds"; design §10 lists nine rows, of which five carry a staleness threshold. Define what counts as a feed for this requirement.
- OBS-04 says failures alert "to the chat"; design §12 says "an admin chat"; SEC-03 permits exactly one allowlisted chat id. Pick one.
- CONS-01 specifies 03:00 without a timezone. TZ-01 makes day boundaries local and TZ=Asia/Dubai, so this is almost certainly 03:00 local — but consolidation reads "the day's messages" while running after midnight, so both the timezone and which day is meant should be explicit.
- P12's done-when says "30 of 30" while OBS-02 says "at least 30 questions". Say 100%, so growing the suite does not weaken the gate.
- MEM-12 and OBS-06 are the same requirement (nightly pg_dump plus markdown fact export) in two phases with different wording. Keep one, reference it from the other.
- PLAN-04's "never planned into busy time" is absolute, but CALR-05 establishes that feed lag makes some busy time invisible. Design §10 words it correctly ("busy time from the calendar feeds"). Scope PLAN-04 to what the feed showed at scheduling time.
- PLAN-09's acceptance ("downloads as a valid zwo file") reads against PLAN-10 ("the coach never generates workout files itself"). Both are satisfiable — the platform renders the zwo — but the acceptance should say so.
- BREAK-01 requires a start and end date; BREAK-04 makes the end date inert for illness breaks. Allow open-ended breaks.
- 23 domains are claimed; there are 23 section headings but 24 ID prefixes (PERF and SEC share the "Non functional" section).
- No requirement covers athlete-initiated deletion — "forget that". `facts.status` has a `rejected` value that nothing in the PRD ever uses. CONS-07's "facts never silently vanish" is about decay, not erasure.
- **Found while applying the fixes, not in the original pass.** Two more instances of the B5 pattern — a companion document offering a remedy the PRD forbids. Setup step 7 says the calendar feed lag "swaps to OAuth without changing anything else in the design", which SEC-04 bans outright; CALR-05 and PLAN-04 are what make the lag survivable instead. And setup §7 sequences step 10 (persona, seeded constraints, starting block) before phase 07, but CHAT-02 and SAFE-01 need the persona and constraints at phase 01 — the step has to split.
- MEM-03 does not require the close-old/insert-new pair to be atomic, which the partial unique index in MEM-02 makes mandatory. Worth stating, since a non-transactional implementation fails intermittently rather than immediately.
- `fact_keys.value_type` exists in the design and design §6 step 3 rejects wrong value types, but no requirement or acceptance covers value-type validation — MEM-01 tests only the foreign key.

## 4. What is notably good

Worth recording so it survives future edits:

- **The governing asymmetry** (§2) is stated once and correctly inherited by ADJ-02, ADJ-03, BLOCK-07 and the design's trigger table. This is the safety-critical property of the system and it is watertight.
- **The absence trap** is handled consistently in four places (FIT-12, RECOV-06, ADJ-08, CHAT-09) plus the design's hard rule. Most specs get this wrong in at least one domain.
- **The body mass section** (HLTH-05 to HLTH-16) is the strongest part of the document — it encodes real measurement uncertainty into requirements, and HLTH-12's "missed readings never count against adherence" is the kind of thing that gets designed out under pressure.
- **"The model proposes, code decides"** (CONS-03, design principle 4) is the right architecture and is stated testably.
- **The local FIT archive as a first-class path** (FIT-14, FIT-15, FIT-16) correctly treats the single upstream as a deletion risk rather than just an availability risk.
